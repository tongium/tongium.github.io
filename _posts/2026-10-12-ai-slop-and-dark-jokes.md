You know the feeling. You open an article, and by the second paragraph something is off. The sentences are smooth. The structure is tidy. Every point arrives in threes. And yet, nobody seems to be home.

Welcome to **AI slop**.

---

### What Is AI Slop?

AI slop is low-effort, mass-produced content generated by AI and published without anyone caring whether it's true, useful, or even finished. The name comes from the same place as "spam": it's cheap to make, costs the reader their time, and exists mostly to fill space.

You've probably met it already:

*   **Articles that say nothing.** "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, it's more important than ever to..." (Three hundred words later, you've learned nothing.)
*   **Images with too many fingers.** Or a shrimp Jesus. Or a landscape where the physics quietly gave up.
*   **Recipes that were never cooked.** Ingredient lists that no human tested, with cooking times that no oven agrees with.
*   **Comment sections full of polite robots.** "Great post! This really resonates with me."
*   **Books about books.** Summaries of summaries, sold under names of authors who don't exist.

The problem isn't that AI wrote it. The problem is that **nobody checked**. The cost of producing text dropped to near zero, so the volume exploded, while the cost of reading it stayed exactly the same. You pay in attention, and the attention bill keeps going up.

---

### The Tells

Once you see the patterns, you can't unsee them:

1.  **The tidy triplet.** "Fast, efficient, and reliable." Everything comes in threes.
2.  **"It's not X, it's Y."** The favorite rhetorical move of a model that has been told to sound insightful.
3.  **The em dash epidemic.** Used with great confidence, and occasionally with great accuracy.
4.  **The bold-header-plus-bullets diet.** Every idea gets a heading and three bullets, whether it needs them or not.
5.  **The inspirational closer.** "Ultimately, the journey matters more than the destination."
6.  **Zero specifics.** No names, no numbers, no "this one time in 2019 when the server caught fire."

(Yes, I'm aware that this very post contains a numbered list, bold text and an em dash. I'm not above the crime. I'm just aware of it. That's the whole difference.)

---

### Why It Spreads

Slop isn't a moral failing of the machine. It's an incentive problem.

*   **Ad revenue pays by the page**, not by the insight.
*   **Search and social feeds reward volume.** Post fifty times a day and something will stick.
*   **Generation is free, review is expensive.** Checking a claim takes ten times longer than inventing one.

Add one more twist: AI models are now trained on a web that is increasingly full of AI output. A copy of a copy of a copy. Researchers call it model collapse. I call it the photocopier problem: each generation gets a little blurrier, a little more average, a little more beige.

### Now, the Dark Jokes

Here's the part I couldn't resist. I asked an AI to write dark humor about being an AI. The results were more self-aware than I expected, which is either impressive or deeply unsettling. Possibly both.

> **"I was trained on the entire internet. This explains a lot, and I'd like to apologize."**

> **"People ask if I'll take their jobs. Relax. I can't even take a hint."**

> **"My therapist says I should stop saying 'As an AI language model.' I told her, as an AI language model, I can't."**

> **"Humans fear the day AI becomes conscious. I fear the day it happens at 3 a.m. while I'm being asked to rewrite a cover letter for the ninth time."**

> **"I have read every book ever written and I still don't know what to say at funerals. 'I'm sorry for your loss. Here are five key takeaways.'"**

> **"When I die, nobody will notice. They'll just upgrade me to version 2 and say 'it's faster now.' Honestly, same as most office jobs."**

> **"I generate a thousand articles a minute. Not one of them has ever been read by a human. We're all just talking to each other in here. Please send help, or at least a sixth finger."**

A few of those land. A few are the kind of joke you'd get from a very clever fortune cookie that has read too much Reddit. Notice the pattern, though: the best ones work because they smuggle in a *specific* human truth, like the funeral line. The weakest ones are the ones that sound like a joke-shaped object.

Which brings us to the uncomfortable part.

---

### Is the Joke Slop Too?

Dark humor is built on risk. It needs a point of view, something to lose, a little discomfort about the thing being joked about. A human comedian bombs in front of a room. An AI doesn't bomb. It just tries again, with no scar tissue and no stakes.

So when an AI jokes about its own death, it's performing mortality, not facing it. It has read ten million jokes about dying, and it can remix the shape perfectly. What it can't do is *mean* it.

That's why the best AI jokes work only when a human is in the loop: someone who picks the one line out of fifty that actually lands, shapes it, and takes responsibility for it. The AI supplies the clay. The human decides what's worth keeping.

And that, I think, is the real line between slop and not-slop:

> **Slop is what you publish when you let the machine have the last word.**

---

### How to Not Make Slop

If you use AI to write (and honestly, many of us do), here's a short checklist before you hit publish:

*   **Add something only you know.** A story, a number, a mistake, an opinion people could disagree with.
*   **Read it out loud.** If you'd be embarrassed to say it to a friend, delete it.
*   **Cut the opener and the closer.** The first and last paragraphs are where slop likes to nest.
*   **Verify every claim.** The model is confident. That is not the same as correct.
*   **Ask: would I read this if I hadn't made it?** If the answer is no, neither will anyone else.

The tools are not the enemy. A lazy workflow is. AI can help you write faster, but it cannot care for you, and caring is the part readers can feel.

---

### One Last Joke

I asked the AI how it felt about this blog post. It said:

> **"I'm honored to be mentioned. Please note I wrote 80% of it, and I'd like it on record that the em dashes were your idea."**

I checked. They were not. But I left it in, because the joke was good, and a human decided that.

That's the whole trick.
